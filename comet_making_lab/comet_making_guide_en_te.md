# Comet-Making Lab: "Dirty Snowball" Nucleus Demonstration
## తోకచుక్క తయారీ ప్రయోగం: మంచు మరియు ధూళి కేంద్రక నమూనా

**Developer:** Dr. Ravi Raja Pothineni (ASTRO / ISRO-YUVIKA Resource Activity)  
**Target Level:** School Students (Grades 5–10), Teacher Training & Public Science Demonstrations

---

### Learning Objective (అభ్యాస లక్ష్యం)
- **English:** Model the composition and physical behaviour of a comet nucleus ("dirty snowball") using dry ice, water, soil, and organic binders, and observe sublimation, outgassing jets, and coma formation directly.
- **తెలుగు:** పొడి మంచు (Dry Ice), నీరు, మట్టి మరియు కర్బన పదార్థాలను ఉపయోగించి తోకచుక్క కేంద్రకం ("ధూళితో కూడిన మంచు బంతి") నిర్మాణాన్ని రూపొందించడం మరియు ఉత్పతనం (sublimation), వాయు జెట్‌లు, కోమా (coma) ఏర్పడే విధానాన్ని ప్రత్యక్షంగా పరిశీలించడం.

---

### Materials Needed (కావలసిన పదార్థాలు)
- **Dry Ice (Solid $\text{CO}_2$, $-78.5^\circ\text{C}$):** ~1.5–2 kg, crushed into fine powder inside a heavy cloth bag (represents frozen carbon dioxide ice in a cometary nucleus)
- **Water:** ~250–300 mL (represents water ice, the primary volatile in comets)
- **Fine Soil / Dark Sand & Charcoal Dust:** 2–3 tablespoons (represents silicate dust and dark carbonaceous surface crust)
- **Corn Syrup / Starch Paste or Vinegar Drop:** 1 tablespoon (represents simple organic compounds detected in cometary nuclei)
- **Heavy-Duty Mixing Bowl, Plastic Liner Bag, Thick Insulated Cryo/Leather Gloves, Safety Goggles, and Wooden Spoon**

---

### Safety Protocol (భద్రతా నియమాలు)
1. **Never touch dry ice with bare skin:** Solid $\text{CO}_2$ is at $-78.5^\circ\text{C}$ and causes severe frostbite on contact. Always wear thick insulated gloves and safety goggles.
2. **Ventilation:** Perform the demonstration in an open outdoor area or a well-ventilated hall so sublimating $\text{CO}_2$ gas disperses safely.
3. **No sealed containers:** Never store dry ice inside an airtight bottle or jar—pressure buildup can rupture the container.

---

### Step-by-Step Procedure
1. **Line the bowl:** Place a sturdy plastic bag inside a large mixing bowl so the frozen nucleus can be lifted out cleanly.
2. **Mix the non-volatile ingredients:** Pour the water into the lined bowl, then stir in the soil, charcoal dust, and corn syrup until a dark muddy suspension forms.
3. **Add crushed dry ice:** Wearing insulated gloves and goggles, gradually add the crushed dry ice while stirring continuously. Fog will pour over the bowl as $\text{CO}_2$ sublimates.
4. **Shape and compact the nucleus:** Gather the plastic liner around the mixture and press firmly with gloved hands for 30–45 seconds until the water freezes the dust and dry ice into a solid, dark, porous chunk.
5. **Unwrap and observe:** Carefully remove the plastic liner and set the model comet nucleus on a tray. Use a flashlight (representing solar radiation) and a gentle hairdryer on a cool setting (representing solar wind/radiation pressure) to examine surface pits and outgassing jets.

---

### Inquiry & Discussion Questions
1. **Sublimation vs. Melting:** Why does the comet nucleus produce visible vapour jets directly from a solid without forming liquid puddles in the vacuum of space?
2. **Dark Crust & Pits:** Look closely at the surface of your model comet—why is it dark and pitted rather than bright white like fresh snow?
3. **Two Tails in Space:** In our classroom model, gravity pulls the cold $\text{CO}_2$ fog downward onto the table. In space, what pushes a real comet's **ion (gas) tail** and **dust tail** away from the Sun?
4. **Meteor Showers:** As a comet repeatedly passes near the Sun and sheds dust grains along its orbit, what happens when Earth crosses that debris trail later in the year (for example, during the **Geminids** or **Perseids**)?
