# Cardboard Diffraction Spectroscope: Build & Observation Lab

**Developer:** Dr. Ravi Raja Pothineni (ASTRO / Novae Astro Club)  
**Target Level:** Middle School, High School & Undergraduate Outreach

## Materials Needed
- Sturdy recycled cardboard box (such as a cereal or shoe box)
- One blank or rewritable CD/DVD (used as a reflection diffraction grating)
- Matte black paper or black tape (to reduce stray internal reflections)
- Razor blade or craft knife (used under adult/instructor supervision) and glue/tape
- Light sources for testing: sunlight reflected off a white sheet (never look directly at the Sun), LED bulb, CFL/fluorescent tube, and sodium/streetlamp if visible

## Assembly Steps
1. **Line the interior:** Line the inside walls of the cardboard box with matte black paper so scattered light does not wash out faint spectral lines.
2. **Cut the slit:** At one end of the box, cut a narrow vertical entrance slit (about 1 mm to 2 mm wide and 3 cm tall). Using two pieces of dark card to form adjustable slit edges gives a cleaner spectrum than cutting freehand.
3. **Mount the grating:** At the opposite end or angled side, cut a viewing slot and mount a piece of CD/DVD at roughly a 45° angle so light entering the slit hits the data track and reflects toward your eye.
4. **Seal light leaks:** Tape all seams closed so light enters only through the narrow slit.

## Student Observation Prompts
- **Continuous vs. Discrete Spectra:** Compare white sunlight (or a warm filament/broad LED) against a fluorescent lamp or discharge source. Which looks like a smooth rainbow band, and which shows distinct bright lines or bands?
- **Angle & Wavelength:** Notice which colour bends more strongly from the diffraction grating—violet/blue (shorter wavelength) or red (longer wavelength)?
- **Why Spectra Matter in Astronomy:** Explain how astronomers use spectral lines to identify chemical elements and Doppler shifts in distant stars and galaxies without visiting them.
